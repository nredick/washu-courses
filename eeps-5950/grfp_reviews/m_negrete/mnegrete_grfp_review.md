## Intellectual Merit

**Intellectual Merit**: the potential to advance knowledge.

1. Is the research question clear and well-motivated? Is the approach well-reasoned and feasible?
   - The research questions are clear and well-outlined, and the presentation structure of the questions followed by the individual tasks with emphasis on the related hypotheses is consistent and easy-to-follow.
   - Starting (rather than ending) the task paragraphs with the hypotheses may make it more clear why the proposed techniques and background information are relevant.
   - Bolder the overall objective in the introduction is very clear, however, eliminating redundant sentences/ideas in the introduction and simplifying vocabulary would help to make the research motivation clearer.
2. Is the applicant well-prepared to carry it out?
   - I imagine alongside a personal statement outlining more of the applicant's background and personal motivation's would help to answer this question. However, the background information and task explanations are well-reasoned and reference both relevant previous works (and why they do not already answer the questions at hand) and reasoning for the proposed methods.

- The mention of economic competitiveness/increased independence may belong in the broader impacts section, rather than IM.

## Broader Impacts

**Broader Impacts**: the potential to benefit society.

1. Are the broader impacts specific, concrete, and connected to the applicant's experience and plans, rather than generic?
   - Yes, the BI are specific and strong; they also cover a range of potential BI from partnerships to society and have varying scales. Bolding is helpful.

## Overall feedback/misc.

- As a non-expert in the field, the chemistry (and some of the geology) vocabulary usage was dense. Wherever possible, it would be helpful to define these terms or use simpler terms/approach the ideas more conceptually.
- Additional comments on writing mechanics and terminology are included in comments directly on the draft.
